## Questions to ask them

Pick two or three. Good questions show you're choosing them too.

**About the role**

1. What would a great first three months look like in this role?
2. What's the biggest challenge the person in this job will face?
3. Is this a new position, or is it replacing someone?

**About the team and company**

4. How would you describe the team I'd be working with?
5. What are the company's main priorities for the next year?
6. How has the team changed over the last year or so?
7. What do the people who do really well here have in common?

**About growth**

8. How do people usually develop or move on from this role?
9. What kind of training or support is there?

**To close**

10. Is there anything about my background that makes you hesitate? I'd like the chance to address it.
11. What are the next steps, and when can I expect to hear from you?

*Avoid in a first interview:* asking only about salary, holidays or working from home, asking something that's on the first page of their website, or saying "No, I think you've covered everything."

## Email templates

**Thank-you email** (send the same day)

::: {.email}
Subject: Thank you, [job title] interview

Hi [name],

Thank you for meeting with me today about the [job title] role. I really enjoyed hearing about [something specific from the conversation].

It confirmed how interested I am. I think my experience with [relevant skill or result] would help with [challenge they mentioned].

If there's anything else you need from me, just let me know. I look forward to hearing about the next steps.

Best regards,
[your name]
:::

**Follow-up** (if the date they gave you has passed)

::: {.email}
Subject: Following up, [job title]

Hi [name],

I hope you're well. I wanted to follow up on my interview on [date] for the [job title] role. I'm still very interested and happy to provide anything else you need.

Do you have an update on the timeline?

Kind regards,
[your name]
:::

**After a rejection** (keep the door open)

::: {.email}
Subject: Re: [job title]

Hi [name],

Thanks for letting me know, and for the time you and the team spent with me. I enjoyed learning about [company], and I'd be glad to be considered for future roles.

If you have a moment, I'd really appreciate any feedback that could help me in my search.

All the best,
[your name]
:::

## Cut-out cheat sheet

Read this in the car park, on the train, or five minutes before the video call.

**The layoff answer:** what happened, why it wasn't about me, where I'm going. 30 seconds. Then stop talking.

**The gap:** why, what I did, why I'm ready now.

**Tell me about yourself:** present, past, future. About a minute. The layoff is one sentence near the end, or not at all.

**Stories:** situation, task, what *I* did, result. Use numbers.

**Never:** blame my old employer, apologise for being laid off, say "I'll take anything," guess why they chose me.

**Always:** pause before answering, be specific, end on the future, ask them two good questions.

**Salary:** a range based on research, not on fear.

**Afterwards:** notes, thank-you email today, keep applying.

*I was good at my job before. I'm still good at it now.*
