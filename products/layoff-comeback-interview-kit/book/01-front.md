# Before you start {.chapter}

You probably bought this book because you have an interview coming up. Maybe it's next week. Maybe it's tomorrow morning and you're reading this at 11 at night with a cold cup of tea next to you.

Either way, there's one moment you keep replaying in your head.

The interview is going fine. You've shaken hands, you've talked about the job, you've even made the interviewer laugh once. Then they glance down at your résumé, look back up, and ask:

*"So what happened at your last job?"*

And your mind goes blank. Or worse, it doesn't go blank. It floods. You start explaining the restructuring, the new director who came in, the way they did it on a Tuesday over a video call, how you'd been there six years, how it wasn't fair. You hear yourself talking for two minutes about something you meant to cover in one sentence. You watch the interviewer's face get politely neutral. And on the drive home you think: *that's where I lost it.*

This book is about that moment, and about everything around it.

I'm not going to tell you that being laid off is a blessing in disguise or that everything happens for a reason. Losing a job is a real loss. It hits your income, your routine, your sense of who you are, and sometimes your pride harder than you expected. If you're angry, that's normal. If you're embarrassed, that's normal too. If you've applied to forty jobs and heard back from three, you're not imagining how hard it is.

What I will tell you is this: **the layoff itself is rarely what costs people the next job. How they talk about it is.**

Interviewers aren't scared of candidates who were laid off. They meet them all the time. Plenty of them have been laid off themselves. What makes them hesitate is a candidate who seems shaken, bitter, evasive, or so eager that it feels like they'd take any job at all. Those are all things you can fix, and most of them you can fix this week.

## How to use this book

If you're **still searching**, or your interview is a few weeks away, follow the **30-day plan** on the next pages. One small task a day, about 30 to 60 minutes each. By day 30 you'll have your answers written, practised and recorded, your stories ready, and a calm head going into any interview that comes up. It's a plan for getting *ready*, not a promise of a job by a certain date. Nobody honest can promise that. But being ready is the part you control, and it's the part that makes the biggest difference.

If your interview is **in a few days**, read the whole book in order. It's short on purpose. Then do the worksheets and practise out loud. I'll keep reminding you to practise out loud, because it's the single thing that separates people who *know* their answer from people who can actually *say* it under pressure.

If your interview is **tomorrow**, read Chapters 2 and 3, write your answer using the worksheet in the Toolkit, and skim Chapter 10. That's about an hour. It'll make a real difference.

One rule before we start. Everything in this book assumes you're telling the truth. I'll help you choose your words, choose your emphasis and leave out what doesn't belong. I won't help you invent a story, because invented stories fall apart on the first follow-up question, and then the problem isn't your layoff anymore. It's your honesty.

The good news is you don't need to invent anything. The truth, said well, works.

# Your 30-day plan {.chapter}

Here's the whole month on four pages. Each day has one main task and the chapter that explains it. Tick it off when it's done. If you miss a day, don't try to catch up by doing three at once. Just pick up where you left off.

Notice that every seventh day is a rest day. That's not a nice extra. Job searching wears people down, and tired people interview badly. Take the rest days seriously.
